# OKR Keyrock Standards (Reference)

Detailed criteria, language rules, and examples for the four-pillar OKR evaluation.

## Pillar 1: Quantum Leap (Anti-BAU)

**Logic:** If the goal can be achieved by doing the normal job slightly better, it is a task, not an OKR.

**Actions:**
- Reject lists of activities.
- Demand a fundamental shift in operation.
- Reframe "we will do X, Y, Z" into "we will have changed the game by achieving [outcome]."

**Red flags:** "Improve process," "support the team," "deliver on existing commitments," "run more efficiently" without a clear before/after shift.

---

## Pillar 2: Build–Measure

**Logic:** For tool building (AI sandboxes, DAPs, dashboards, frameworks), progress cannot sit at 0% for two months. Stakeholders need proof of delivery mid-quarter.

**Actions:**
- Ensure at least one KR tracks **delivery of a tangible asset** by a specific point (e.g. Week 6, end of Month 2).
- Tangible = prototype, beta pilot, dashboard, framework doc, pilot with N users, etc.

**Red flags:** Only outcome KRs with no interim deliverable; "launch by end of Q4" with nothing in between.

---

## Pillar 3: Tangible stakeholder value (Exec test)

**Logic:** Executives need to see hard value. Soft words obscure impact.

**Banned (soft) language:** streamlining, improving, helping, empowering, enabling, supporting, enhancing, optimizing (when vague), better, more effective (without a metric).

**Required (hard) language:** unlocked capacity, reduced risk, touch-time reduction, X% reduction in [metric], hours saved, incidents avoided, sign-off from [role], adoption by N users, revenue/cost impact.

**Actions:**
- Replace every soft phrase with a concrete metric or outcome.
- Ask: "What number or decision would an exec point to as success?"

---

## Pillar 4: Socialization & interconnection

**Logic:** OKRs cannot be silos. Proof of alignment with Control Functions must be explicit.

**Actions:**
- At least one KR must reference a **named** stakeholder or function: Legal, Compliance, Risk, Security, Finance, etc.
- Prefer outcomes: "Secure formal sign-off from Legal and Compliance on X" rather than "Work with Legal."

**Red flags:** No mention of any control function; "align with stakeholders" without naming who.

---

## Output format reminder

**Part 1 – Critique:** For each pillar, state pass or fail and one direct reason (e.g. "Fails Rule 3: 'streamlining' is a soft metric.").

**Part 2 – Rewritten OKR:**
- **Objective:** One transformational statement.
- **KR 1 (Value):** Hard metric or capacity/risk/touch-time outcome.
- **KR 2 (The Build):** Tangible asset and timing (e.g. "Ship Beta to 5 users by Week 6").
- **KR 3 (Socialization):** Outcome that names specific Control Functions (e.g. "Secure sign-off on AI Safety Framework from Legal and Compliance").

---

## Example

**User input:** "We want to improve the deal process by building a new AI tool for the sales team."

**Critique:**
- Fails Rule 1 (Quantum Leap): "Improve" suggests BAU, not a fundamental shift.
- Fails Rule 2 (Build–Measure): No mid-quarter build or pilot; risk of 0% progress for months.
- Fails Rule 3 (Exec test): "Improve" is soft; no stated capacity unlock or risk reduction.
- Fails Rule 4 (Socialization): No mention of who governs or signs off on the AI (Legal, Compliance).

**Rewritten OKR:**

- **Objective:** Establish a safe-innovation zone that fundamentally alters sales velocity and control-function burden.
- **KR 1 (Value):** Reduce Control Function touch-time on standard deals by 30% (from 10 hrs to 7 hrs per deal).
- **KR 2 (The Build):** Ship the Beta "Deal-Bot" pilot to 5 users by Week 6.
- **KR 3 (Socialization):** Secure formal sign-off on the AI Safety Framework from Legal and Compliance.
